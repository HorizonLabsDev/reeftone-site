Underwater color correction for GoPro footage

Works with any camera

Underwater footage from a GoPro

A GoPro records what reaches the lens. Below a few meters, red and orange are already missing from the light, so even good GoPro footage looks blue or green. Color correction brings those colors back afterwards, on your iPhone.

How to correct GoPro footage with Reef Tone

  1. Copy your clips to your iPhone so they appear in Photos, for example with GoPro's own app, or save them to Files.
  2. Open Reef Tone and import the video from Photos or Files.
  3. Choose the water: Blue or Green, depending on what the water looked like.
  4. Set the intensity and compare with the original before you save.
  5. Save the result to Photos or Files.

Reef Tone works with any video you can import from Photos or Files, so it is not tied to one camera. GoPro clips are handled like any other video. It has been tested with footage from DJI cameras. Everything is processed on your iPhone, with no account and no upload.

Tips for GoPro underwater clips

  • Pick the water type per dive. A blue reef and a green lake need different settings.
  • If you use a red filter, the footage starts with more warm color, so a lower intensity is often enough. Compare with the original.
  • Get close and use the light. Shallow water and sunshine keep the most color, and video lights help below about 10 m.
  • Keep your originals. Reef Tone saves the corrected video only when you choose to save it.
  • Process several clips together with batch processing in Reef Tone Pro (up to 10 videos at once).
