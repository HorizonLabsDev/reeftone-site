How to fix green underwater video

A short guide for divers and snorkelers

Why underwater video turns green

Water absorbs red first, then orange and yellow. Green and blue travel the farthest, so footage shifts toward blue-green with depth. Whether it ends up more blue or more green depends on what is in the water. Algae, plankton and suspended particles, as in lakes, quarries and many coastal seas, push the cast toward green. Very clear open water tends to look blue.

The cast is not a camera error. The warm colors are missing from the light before they reach the lens.

Fix green water with Reef Tone

  1. Import your video from Photos or Files.
  2. When Reef Tone asks how the water was, choose Green. The correction then adapts to green water.
  3. Set the intensity and compare with the original before you save.
  4. If you want more control, switch to advanced mode for saturation, contrast and dehazing.
  5. Save the result to Photos or Files.

Everything is processed on your iPhone. There is no account and nothing is uploaded.

Tips for green water

  • Choose the water type that matches what you saw. If the water looked green, pick Green, even if the footage also has some blue.
  • Start low and raise the intensity gradually. Compare with the original and stop when skin tones, fish and rocks look natural.
  • Get closer. Less water between you and the subject means less cast to remove.
  • Avoid stirred-up silt. Particles in the water scatter light and reduce contrast, which dehazing can help with.
  • Use video lights when you can. They bring back warm colors at the source.
